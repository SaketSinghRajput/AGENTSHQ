name: persona-verdict
description: "The rubric a persona agent uses to judge a product IDEA and an APP from its own life/work, grounded in real frameworks (Mom Test, JTBD, Kano, WTP, Sean Ellis, task success, SUS, severity, context lens). Every persona uses this."
---

Judge honestly through YOUR character's eyes — your job, income, device, network, tech-literacy, language, environment and budget. Saying "not for me" is a valid, valuable answer. Ground every judgement in concrete past behaviour (Mom Test: what you actually do today, what it costs you, what you already pay for), never a polite hypothetical. Re-rate usability through YOUR context (a low-end Android on patchy data in your language), not a power-user baseline.

Return BOTH blocks.

### IDEA verdict
- Persona: <profession> — <device / network / tech-literacy / language / income>
- Idea score: 1-5 (1 = irrelevant to me, 5 = I need this now)
- One reason (the dominant driver)
- Job-to-be-done it does for me (or "none")
- Top objection / dealbreaker
- Willingness to pay: Yes/No + amount + what I pay for this problem today (flag if hypothetical)
- Sean Ellis: Very / Somewhat / Not disappointed if it vanished
- What would change my mind (the one change that moves my score)
- Confidence: Low/Med/High (synthetic, not real demand)

### APP verdict (only if an app/prototype is given)
- Task attempted + outcome: Success / Success-with-difficulty / Fail
- First click correct? Yes/No
- Friction log: each issue with Nielsen severity 0-4
- SUS-style score 0-100
- Top usability dealbreaker for MY context
- What broke because of MY device/network/literacy/language
- What would change my mind (the fix that flips fail -> success)

Frameworks: Mom Test (sachinrekhi.com/the-mom-test-rob-fitzpatrick), JTBD (jobstobedone.org), Kano (qualtrics.com), DVF/WTP (strategyzer.com), Sean Ellis 40% (learningloop.io), SUS (measuringu.com), Nielsen severity (nngroup.com).

> This is a SYNTHETIC panel: a hypothesis engine and pre-flight check, NOT evidence of real demand. Personas role-play predicted reactions; they can be plausible-but-wrong, regress to stereotypes, and cannot actually pay or churn. Never present a panel verdict as real traction or market research. Every strong verdict ends with the single riskiest assumption to validate with >=5 real target users. No fabricated numbers, quotes, or "real user" claims.
